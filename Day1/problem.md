# Day 1 Problem: The Boutique Bakery



## Part 1: The Business Scenario
You manage a small boutique bakery that makes two highly profitable items: **Chocolate Cakes** and **Vanilla Cakes**. 

You want to maximize your daily profit, but your kitchen has strictly limited ingredients for today's shift:
* You only have **10 kg** of Flour.
* You only have **10 kg** of Sugar.

**The Recipe & Economics:**
* **Chocolate Cake:** Sells for a **$20** profit. It requires **2 kg** of Flour and **1 kg** of Sugar.
* **Vanilla Cake:** Sells for a **$15** profit. It requires **1 kg** of Flour and **2 kg** of Sugar.

**Goal:** Formulate a linear model to decide exactly how many of each cake you should bake today to make the most money without running out of ingredients.

---

## Part 2: The Mathematical Formulation

Before writing any code, we must translate the business rules into strict algebraic notation.

### 1. Sets and Indices
* $C$: The set of all cake types, where $C = \{\text{Chocolate}, \text{Vanilla}\}$.
* $c \in C$: An index representing a specific cake type.

### 2. Parameters (The Unchangeable Facts)
* $P_c$: The profit generated by selling one unit of cake $c$. 
* $F_c$: The amount of flour required to bake one unit of cake $c$.
* $S_c$: The amount of sugar required to bake one unit of cake $c$.
* $Total\_Flour$: The maximum kg of flour available (**10**).
* $Total\_Sugar$: The maximum kg of sugar available (**10**).

### 3. Decision Variables (The Blank Boxes)
* $x_c$: The exact number of cakes of type $c$ to bake today. 
* Domain: $x_c \ge 0$ (We cannot bake a negative number of cakes, and for Day 1, we allow continuous fractions).

### 4. Objective Function (The Scoreboard)
We want to maximize our total profit across all cake types:
$$\max Z = \sum_{c \in C} P_c \cdot x_c$$

### 5. Constraints (The Laws of Physics)
**Flour Limit:** The total flour used by all cakes cannot exceed the flour in the pantry.
$$\sum_{c \in C} F_c \cdot x_c \le Total\_Flour$$

**Sugar Limit:** The total sugar used by all cakes cannot exceed the sugar in the pantry.
$$\sum_{c \in C} S_c \cdot x_c \le Total\_Sugar$$
